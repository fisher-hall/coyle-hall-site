A community of Tradition

Building a Legacy

The story of Coyle began back in 1952, when Sally Fisher donated the money to build Fisher Hall in memory of her late husband, Fred Fisher, a former member of the Board of Trustees. The dorm was originally constructed to house 156 seniors, and had a large number of single rooms. At the time, it was one of the first dorms at Notre Dame to host newer ammenities like shared common lounges, and it was home to the original St. Paul's Chapel. The dorm was renovated a number of times, most notably in the Summer of 2001.

Throughout its glorious existence, the Fisher/Coyle Community has been home to some of Notre Dame's most legendary alumni. NBA stars LaPhonso Ellis ’92 and Orlando Woolridge ’81 once walked these halls, as well as the one and only Regis Philbin ’53. In addition to the legend among legends, Joe Montana ’79, hundreds of students, athletes, businessmen, engineers, scientists, artists, and leaders have called Fisher Hall home.

Keeping it Alive

In 2024, the University announced that the old Fisher Hall would be demolished to make way for a new residence hall, Coyle Hall, named in honor of John and Jill Coyle. It was announced that, while the new building was constructred, the community would live in Zahm Hall under the name "Fisher Community in Zahm Hall". Cast out of their beloved South Quad home, the community embraced the change, determined to keep the spirit of Fisher alive. Relationships with new dorms were developed, and the community claimed a new dorm table, this time in North Dining Hall. Life in Zahm brought small upgrades as well, with an increased number of small lounges, larger rooms, a larger chapel, and a newly renovated basement in gym. Still, the community longed to be back in their original home, and excitement for the new building grew.

Moving Forward

In Summer 2025, the community adopted a new name for the year: "Coyle Community in Zahm Hall". This was the start of a new relationship between the dorm community and the donors, John and Jill, and offered the chance to make new traditions and memories. Many traditions, like Fisher Funk, were rebranded to Coyle equivilents, like the Coyle Crunk, and many new traditions were started, all bearing names that began with 'C'.

Finally, in Fall 2026, the community moved into the brand new Coyle Hall. Featuring a spacious lounges, kitchens on every floor, new study areas, six-man rooms, a newer, taller, and bigger St. Paul's Chapel, and, at long last, air conditioning In 2024, the University announced that the old Fisher Hall would be demolished to make way for a new residence hall, Coyle Hall, named in honor of John and Jill Coyle. Set to open in Fall 2026, the new hall will house 258 students, and will feature a large chapel, common spaces, study rooms, kitchens, laundry facilities and a gym, as well as air conditioning. As the dorm is being built, the Coyle Community is residing in Zahm Hall for the 2024-2025 and 2025-2026 school years.

While the dorm may be experiencing new changes, the spirit and sense of community in Coyle is stronger than ever. Despite a new name and building, the classic traditions and events of the Fisher Community will transition to Coyle, and as new mottos, mascots, and traditions are made, new students will have an opportunity to shape the legacy of the dorm for years to come.
